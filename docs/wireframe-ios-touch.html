Making the phone build thumb-sized

The note list has had its phone pass and now clears 44 pt. Everything a person touches while writing has not: the formatting palette is 31 × 29, its menus are 27 pt rows, and the Aa popover bottoms out around 18 pt with 11 px labels. This wireframe measures what is actually shipping today, sets the rules, and draws the remaining surfaces before and after.

Measured from src/app/styles/app.css and src/app/ui/window-chrome.ts at the max-width: 620px and 720px breakpoints — the two queries that define the phone layout. Sizes below are the authored CSS values, not estimates. Reference: Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Layout & Accessibility (44 pt minimum hit target); iOS system patterns for keyboard accessory bars and sheets.

The palette is also missing its commands. At max-width: 720px the rules .styles-bar-overflow and .styles-asset are set to display: none, which removes Todo, Bold, Italic, Link, Tables and Image from the phone row entirely. What is left is Headers, Lists, Highlight, More, Aa and the word count. Bold and italic — the two most-used marks in any editor — are two taps deep inside More. app.css:3609 · window-chrome.ts:851
Why the row cannot simply scroll today. The palette's menus open upward out of the bar, so overflow-x on the row would clip them — a box with one axis clipped clips the other. That constraint is what forces the overflow rule above. Moving those menus to bottom sheets removes the constraint, and then nothing has to be hidden. app.css:3677–3690 — the comment that documents the trap

2The rules

Seven rules. Everything drawn after this follows them, and each one is checkable in review.

R1 — 44 × 44 floor. Every control a finger can reach is at least 44 pt in both axes. No exceptions on the phone, including rows inside menus and sheets.
R2 — hit area ≥ ink. The target is 44 pt; the drawing need not be. A 22 pt icon centred in a 44 pt button keeps the app's quiet look while doubling what you can hit. This is what stops the redesign from turning SimpleMark chunky.
R3 — 8 pt between neighbours. Adjacent targets need clearance or 44 pt is a lie: a fingertip covers roughly 10 mm, and today's palette has 2 pt gaps.
R4 — 17 pt body, 15 pt secondary, nothing below 13. Today's sheet rows are 12 px and the Aa popover is 11 px. Sizes come from Dynamic Type, so a person who has raised their system text size gets it.
R5 — menus become sheets. A dropdown anchored to a 31 pt button is a desktop idiom. On the phone every menu is a bottom sheet with 52 pt rows, opening from the thumb end of the screen. The Library sheet already does exactly this, at exactly 52 pt. It is the pattern; the palette menus and the note context menu are what still have to adopt it.
R6 — nothing depends on hover. The list already got this right: .note-pin and .note-close are forced to opacity: 1 at phone width rather than waiting for a :hover that never arrives. The rule is that no new affordance may be hover-gated — this webview does not report (hover: none) honestly, so a tap can leave a stuck hover state. app.css:2137 · window-chrome.ts:2828 — the comment that found it
R7 — respect the safe areas. Bottom chrome is padded by env(safe-area-inset-bottom); nothing sits under the home indicator. Already true of the palette and figure viewer — extend it to every sheet.
What already works and must not be lost. The keyboard-riding palette position (--keyboard-inset measured from visualViewport), the two-screen phone navigation with a named back button and an edge-swipe counterpart, full-height swipe actions on note rows, the Library-as-sheet at 52 pt, and the whole 44 pt list pass. This wireframe continues that work into the writing surfaces; it does not redo it.

Sheet rules

  • 52 pt rows, 17 pt text. Comfortably above the floor, and the same measurement everywhere — palette menus, note context menu, the Library sheet.
  • Grabber and Done. Two ways out, both at the thumb end. Tapping outside also dismisses.
  • It replaces the keyboard, not the document. Opening a sheet dismisses the keyboard, so the sheet has room and the selection stays put — the palette's existing mousedown suppression already keeps focus in the editor.
  • Checkmark, not colour reversal. The current row uses a full-bleed accent fill; a trailing ✓ reads at a glance and survives a dark-mode audit.
  • This is the change that unblocks scrolling. With no upward menus, the palette row can take overflow-x: auto, and the six hidden commands come back.

Landed, and what is left

  • Already 44 × 44. Search and new note in a 62 pt header, with 20 pt icons and a 44 pt title target that opens the Library sheet.
  • Swipe already reveals full-height 76 pt actions, forced to opacity: 1 at this width so nothing waits on a hover that never comes. Icon-only — labels would be the one improvement left, and they are cosmetic.
  • Residual: the search field is still 34 pt with 13 px text. It has no phone override at all, so it kept its desktop size while everything around it grew. Drawn here as a permanent 44 pt field rather than a mode you enter — which also retires the search/close toggle.
  • Residual: the note context menu is still a 27 pt dropdown with 13 px rows. It is the one menu on this screen that did not become a sheet. Same treatment as §3's menus: 52 pt rows, 17 pt text.
  • Open question, not a defect: the large-title shape. Moving the collection name below the bar as a scrolling large title is the standard iOS list, but the current 62 pt header is compact and already passes. Worth doing only if the nav bar gains other actions.
  • Open question: new note in the thumb arc. The most frequent action in the app sits in the hardest corner to reach one-handed. A 56 pt button in the bottom right is the standard answer; the cost is that it floats over the list. Both controls could coexist.

What changes

  • Twenty-odd controls in 232 pt of width. Four-across button rows compute to roughly 52 pt wide and 26 pt tall, with 3 pt gaps and 11 px labels.
  • The size steppers are the smallest thing in the app at about 20 pt tall, and they sit next to 30 × 24 theme swatches.
  • Text size gets the whole width as a 44 pt stepper with the value stated. It is the control people come here for; it should not be a 20 pt afterthought beside the swatches.
  • Font family becomes a segmented control — 44 pt, three across, each rendered in its own face so the choice shows itself.
  • Swatches go to 44 × 44 with a selection ring rather than a border change.
  • Line height and measure become disclosure rows. They are set once and rarely revisited, so they cost one row here and open their own 44 pt options — instead of six 22 pt buttons crowding the controls people use daily.
Paragraph and inline styles leave this sheet. Title/H1/H2/H3, Body/Mono/Quote/Code and B/I/U/S are document commands, not reader settings — they belong in the palette (§3), which now has room for them. Aa keeps only what changes how the page reads.

How this gets verified. A Playwright check at 393 × 852 that walks every element matching button, [role="button"], a inside the phone layout and asserts getBoundingClientRect() is ≥ 44 in both axes, with an explicit allowlist for anything genuinely exempt. That turns R1 from a design intention into a gate — the same move tests/ui/perf-budget.ts made for wall-clock.
Two things this wireframe does not settle. First, whether the accessory bar should be a real inputAccessoryView on the Swift side rather than a webview element riding --keyboard-inset — the CSS comment says a webview cannot ask for one, and that is worth re-testing before committing to the measured approach. Second, Dynamic Type: honouring the system text size means the 44 pt floor becomes a floor rather than a fixed size, and rows must grow. Both are their own decisions.
